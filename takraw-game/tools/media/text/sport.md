# Sepak takraw: the sport

Sepak takraw is a net game played with the feet. Its name joins the Malay word *sepak*, to kick, and the Thai
*takraw*, the woven ball. Two teams face each other across a net on a court the size of a badminton doubles court,
13.4 by 6.1 metres, the net 1.52 metres high for men and 1.42 for women. They have three touches a side to send the
ball back over, using the feet, knees, chest and head, never the hands or arms.

Kicking a woven rattan ball to keep it in the air is centuries old in Southeast Asia: *sepak raga* in Malaysia,
*takraw* in Thailand, *sipa* in the Philippines, *chinlone* in Myanmar, *kataw* in Laos. The game over a net, with
its rules and its name agreed between the region's countries around 1960, grew from these. It has been played at the
Southeast Asian Games since the 1960s and at the Asian Games since 1990, and the International Sepak Takraw
Federation (ISTAF) governs it worldwide.

In the regu, three a side, the *tekong* serves from a circle at the back, the *feeder* sets the ball high by the net,
and the *striker* attacks, often with a roll spike, flipping over in the air to kick the ball down. Thailand leads
the sport, with Malaysia, Indonesia, Myanmar, Vietnam and the Philippines close behind, and it is played from Japan
and Korea to India and beyond. Today's balls are usually synthetic, woven like the rattan ones.
