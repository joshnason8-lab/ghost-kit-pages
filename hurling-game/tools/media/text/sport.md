# The sport: hurling

Hurling is an Irish field game, and one of the oldest in the world. It appears in Ireland's early laws and in its
legends, where the young Cú Chulainn wins his name with a hurley and ball, and it has been played across the country
for well over a thousand years. Its rules were first written down in 1884, when the Gaelic Athletic Association was
founded to organise it alongside Gaelic football, and the game has been played by those rules, revised over the
years, ever since.

Two teams of fifteen play on a field up to 145 metres long, with H-shaped goalposts at each end. Each player carries a
hurley (camán), a flat-bladed stick of ash, and the ball is the sliotar, about the size of a tennis ball, with a cork
core and a leather cover. Players strike it in the air or along the ground, catch it, and run with it balanced or
bounced on the hurley (the solo). Sending it over the crossbar between the posts scores a point; into the net under
the crossbar, past the goalkeeper, a goal, worth three points.

Hurling is fast, skilful and physical, and every player wears a helmet with a face guard. It is played by amateurs,
for their parish club and their county, and its championship is one of the great events of the Irish summer. The
women's game, played to very similar rules, is camogie.
