# Pok Ta Pok Rivals

**The hip ballgame of Mexico, as it is played.**

## Short description

Pok Ta Pok Rivals is the Mesoamerican ballgame on your phone. Strike a heavy rubber ball with your hip, the only
part of the body allowed, and drive it past the other town's end. Play ulama de cadera, the living game of Sinaloa,
or a reconstruction of the ancient stone court and its rings.

## Long description

People in Mesoamerica have played with rubber balls for more than three thousand years. The game survives today as
ulama, kept alive in a few villages of southern Sinaloa, Mexico. Teams there send a solid rubber ball of three to
four kilograms back and forth along a long strip of packed earth, with the hip and nothing else. In the Yucatán and
Central America, teams have revived it as pok-ta-pok, or pelota maya.

Pok Ta Pok Rivals plays the way the sport does. Your left thumb moves your player. You tap HIP just before the
heavy ball reaches you, and the timing decides whether it flies deep, skids low, or goes astray. When it comes in
low or rolling, you DIVE and meet it on the ground. Ulama's real count is here: only one side holds it at a time,
rayas move it up and down, and in urria a whole count can be wiped out. The first side to eight wins.

The classic stone court is a reconstruction, built from the old chroniclers' accounts. It is an I-shaped alley
between sloping walls, with a stone ring set high in each wall. A ball through a ring wins the game outright, and
almost never happens. When it does, you see it again in a replay. On the stone courts the serve comes down to
bounce in front of the other side, as pelota maya is played in Yucatán today. Those games open with an offering
of copal, shown respectfully, which you can skip.

Play a single match, run a town tournament of eight towns to the final, or watch two towns played by the computer.
You can control the whole team or stay one player all match. The players and townspeople are realistic figures,
and their running and serving are drawn from motion capture. There are four courts, sixteen towns with their own
colours, six languages and graphics levels for everyday phones. The game is fully offline, with no ads, no
purchases and no data collected.

It is an independent game, made with respect for a living tradition and its keepers. Towns appear by their place
names only, and every player is invented.
