# Features

- **The real hip game.** Ulama de cadera as played in southern Sinaloa today: the hip only, the heavy ball, the analco
  and the chivo.
- **The real count.** One side holds the count, rayas swing it up and down, urria can wipe it out, and the first side
  to eight wins.
- **The classic stone court.** The ancient game reconstructed from the chronicles, with sloping walls, end courts
  and a ring in each wall that wins outright, replayed in slow motion when it goes in.
- **Pelota maya's serve and offering.** On the stone courts the serve bounces in front of the other side, as in
  Yucatán today, and the games open with an offering of copal, shown respectfully, which you can skip.
- **Three ways to play.** Play a match, take a town through an eight-town knock-out tournament, or watch two towns
  played by the computer.
- **Whole team or one player.** Control whichever of your players goes for the ball, or stay one player all match.
- **Opponents at three levels.** Village, Regional and Champion: from slow, wayward strikes to low, hard returns
  you have to dive for.
- **Four courts.** A Sinaloa village at dusk, a festival court by day, a stone court in the Yucatán forest, and the
  same court by torchlight.
- **Sixteen towns.** From Sinaloa, the Yucatán, Quintana Roo, Chiapas, Guatemala, Belize and Honduras, each with its
  own nickname and colours.
- **Realistic players and crowds.** Lifelike players and townspeople, with a run and a serve from motion capture.
- **The sound of the game.** The thud of the ball and the hip, bare feet on earth and stone, the crowd, the forest
  at dusk and the torches at night, and menu music of cane flute, drums and rattles.
- **For everyday phones.** Low, Medium and High graphics, an automatic step down if a match runs slowly, and a
  battery saver.
- **Controls that fit your hands.** A floating joystick, big buttons in three sizes that you can move, a
  left-handed layout, optional help that moves your player to the ball, and vibration you can turn off.
- **Six languages.** Spanish, English, Portuguese, French, German and Italian, with How to Play explaining the
  rules, the count and the story of the game.
- **Offline and private.** No internet needed or used, no ads, no purchases, no account and no data collected.
