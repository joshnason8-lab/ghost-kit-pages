== App name ==
Pok Ta Pok Rivals

== Short description ==
The hip ballgame of Mexico: ulama, the heavy rubber ball, the count to eight.

== Full description ==
Pok Ta Pok Rivals is the Mesoamerican ballgame on your phone, played as it is still played today. Strike a heavy rubber ball with your hip across the analco of a long earth court, read its low bounce, dive for it as it skids along the ground, and drive it past the other town's end.

THE LIVING GAME: ULAMA DE CADERA
• The game still played in the villages of southern Sinaloa, Mexico: four or five a side on the taste, a strip of packed earth 60 metres long and 4 wide.
• The hip only. Touch the ball with anything else and the raya goes to the other side.
• A raya is won when the ball dies in the other half, goes past their end line, or they knock it out.
• The real count: one side holds it, rayas go up and down, and in urria a whole count can be wiped out. First to eight.
• The male serves: rolled along the earth (abajo) or thrown high (arriba).
• Two judges, the veedores, at the analco call every raya.

THE CLASSIC COURT (RECONSTRUCTED)
An I-shaped stone court with sloped walls, rebuilt from the old chronicles: the ball plays off the stone, and a stone ring set high in each wall wins the game outright. It almost never happens.

WAYS TO PLAY
• Play a match: pick a court and two towns.
• Town tournament: eight towns, knock-out, to the final.
• Watch a match between two towns played by the computer.

FOUR COURTS
A village court in Sinaloa at dusk, a festival court by day, a stone court in the Yucatán forest under a pyramid, and the same court by torchlight.

SIXTEEN TOWNS
From Sinaloa, the Yucatán, Quintana Roo, Chiapas, Guatemala, Belize and Honduras, each with its own nickname and colours.

ON THE COURT
• Lifelike players and townspeople, with a run and a serve taken from motion capture.
• On the stone courts the serve comes down to bounce in front of the other side, who play it off the bounce, as pelota maya is played in Yucatán today.
• Before games there, an elder carries smoking copal among the players while a conch sounds, as those games begin today. A tap skips it.
• A ball through the ring gets its replay.
• Play as the whole team, or be one player for the whole match.

MADE FOR YOUR PHONE
• A floating joystick and big buttons, left-handed layout, button sizes, and buttons you can move.
• Runs on everyday phones: graphics that set themselves, and a battery saver.
• In Spanish, English, Portuguese, French, German and Italian.
• How to Play explains the rules, the count and the story of the game and its words.

No ads, no in-app purchases, no account, no data collected.

Pok Ta Pok Rivals is an independent game, not connected with or endorsed by any sporting body, federation, tournament or club. Towns appear by their place names only; every player is invented.
