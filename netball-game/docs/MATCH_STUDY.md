# Match study: what a real international looks like, and what the game takes from it

The owner sent a full broadcast of an 80-minute international (four 15-minute quarters, a sports hall arena, about
2,000 seats). It was watched as contact sheets (a frame every 10 seconds, the whole match) and as one-frame-a-second
runs through centre passes, circle play, goals and the breaks. Only how the game is played, umpired, staged and shown
is taken from it: no teams, players, kits, sponsors or names.

## How the players move

| Seen | What it means for the game |
|---|---|
| At a centre pass the attackers wait on the transverse line, spread across the court (GA toward the middle, WA wide), each with her defender pressed to her goal-side shoulder; the C in the circle; the receiving C a stride outside the centre third line. The moment the whistle goes they **drive** (two or three hard strides) and the first pass goes to whoever got free, usually the WA or GA at the top of the centre third. | Centre pass set-up kept; on the whistle the attackers' first lead is a straight, flat-out drive into the centre third, not a random spot. |
| After a goal nobody sprints: the scorers walk back, players **jog** to their centre-pass spots, a few seconds of reset. | Players walk/jog to the next centre pass (only the restart's C hurries). |
| In the circle the **GS holds**: back to her keeper, feet wide, close to the post, one arm out where she wants the ball. The **GK front-marks** her a lot (stands between ball and shooter, arms up), and switches to behind when the ball is at the circle edge. | GS posts (holds still, arm out) rather than wandering; GK and GD front the shooter when the ball is outside the circle and drop goal-side when it is on the edge. |
| Feeders (WA, C) work **the circle edge**: in the pocket at the corner of the circle and the top of it, shuffling sideways to find a passing lane; the GA moves in and out of the circle. | WA and C anchors sit on the circle edge when the ball is in the goal third, sliding side to side. |
| On a shot: the keeper's arms go straight up over the ball from 0.9 m, the others **turn to the post and box out** for the rebound (GS and GA inside, GK and GD outside). | Rebound positions round the post during every shot. |
| Defenders off the ball **zone** the space in front of the circle as much as mark one player; on a loose ball the two nearest race for it. | Defenders off the ball shade toward the circle when the ball is in their goal third. |

## The umpires

- Two, in a light teal shirt and dark shorts, each with one half for the whole match.
- Each walks her **side line** level with play and goes **round the corner onto the goal line** when the ball is in
  her circle, so she sees the shot and the contact from the end.
- After a call she stops, blows, holds the signal with an arm out (the direction of the award), then walks on.
- She jogs, never sprints, and stands still for long spells at the transverse line when play is in the centre third.

## The venue and the people round the court

- **LED boards** along both side lines, a band at knee height scrolling short messages and colours; it changes every
  few seconds.
- **Floor decals** (a logo in the run-off by the side lines) and painted end walls in the home team colour, a
  basketball board winched up behind each post, and a lit box on each goal post (the goal light).
- **Benches** behind the end lines and at the scorers' table: the reserves sit in a row in team tracksuits, the coaches
  stand at the end of the bench; officials at the table with laptops.
- **The stands** full along both sides, thinner at the ends; people stand at a goal.
- **At every break** both teams **huddle**: the seven in a tight ring round their coach near their bench, the reserves
  with them, drinks bottles; the umpires walk to the middle.

## The broadcast

- A high camera on the side, panning with the ball; **cuts to a close shot of the post for every shot**, then back.
- A **full-screen team card** (two halves in team colours, crests, names, the venue) before the match and at half
  time; the scoreboard (top left) shows the two short names, the scores, the quarter and the clock.
