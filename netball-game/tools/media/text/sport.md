# What is netball?

Netball is a fast, non-contact team sport for two teams of seven. It grew out of early basketball: in the 1890s the
new American game was brought to England and adapted in women's colleges, and in the early 1900s it gained its own
rules and its own name. It spread through schools and clubs across the Commonwealth; an international federation
(today World Netball) was founded in 1960, and the first world championship was held in England in 1963.

The court is divided into thirds, and each of the seven positions, from Goal Shooter to Goal Keeper, may go only into
her own areas, shown by the letters on her bib. A player may not run with the ball: she lands, may pivot on her
landing foot, and must pass or shoot within three seconds. Defenders must stay 0.9 m from the player with the ball,
and there is no contact. Only the Goal Shooter and Goal Attack may score, and only from inside the goal circle, by
putting the ball through a ring 3.05 m high with no backboard. Every goal is followed by a centre pass, taken by the
two teams in turn, and a match is four quarters of fifteen minutes.

The result is a game of constant movement, sharp passing and split-second timing. It is played by millions of people,
most of all in Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, Jamaica, South Africa, Malawi, Uganda and across the
Pacific and the Caribbean, and it has been part of the Commonwealth Games since 1998.
