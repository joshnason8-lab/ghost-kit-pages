## What is hurling?

Hurling is an Irish field game for two teams of 15. Players use a wooden stick called a **hurley** (*camán* in Irish) to strike a small leather ball called a **sliotar**.

You can hit the ball on the ground or in the air, catch it in your hand, and run with it balanced on the hurley. Strikes are fast: a well-hit sliotar can travel more than 110 m.

Teams score by sending the ball over the crossbar for a **point** or under it into the net for a **goal**, worth three points. The game is run by the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA). Its players are amateurs, including at the top level.

In 2018 UNESCO added hurling to its list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The listing also covers camogie, the women's game.

## History and origins

### Ancient roots, legend and evidence

Hurling is very old, but its exact age is debated. UNESCO describes it as dating back about 2,000 years. Some writers claim three thousand years or more, but that figure rests on mythology, not evidence.

**Legend:** Irish mythological texts place a hurling game at the Battle of Moytura (*Cath Maige Tuired*). The best-known legend is in the *Táin* cycle. In it the boy hero Cúchulainn (Cú Chulainn) plays hurling at Emain Macha. These are stories, not historical records.

**Written evidence:** Early Irish (Brehon) law texts mention the game. Commentaries on the *Seanchas Mór* say a king's son could have a hurley hooped with bronze, while other boys had copper. Another law tract, *Meallbretha*, deals with compensation for injuries in games, most of them like hurling.

**Physical evidence:** The earliest archaeological finds of hurling balls date from the later 12th century.

### Bans, gentry matches and a new association

Colonial authorities tried more than once to restrict the game. The Statute of Kilkenny (1366) banned "horlinge" among the English settlers. A 1527 statute in Galway also restricted the "little ball" game played with hooked sticks.

The 18th century is often called hurling's golden age. Landowners organised big matches between parishes and counties. Two styles existed: a northern winter game (*camán*), where you could not handle the ball, and a southern summer game (*iomán* or *báire*), where you could.

The Gaelic Athletic Association was founded on 1 November 1884 in Hayes' Hotel, Thurles, County Tipperary. Michael Cusack, one of its founders, wrote modern rules based mainly on the southern game.

The first All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship was played in 1887. Its final was played on 1 April 1888 in Birr. Thurles, representing Tipperary, won it.

### Sister games

**Camogie** is the women's version of hurling. Its first matches were played in 1904 and its association was founded in 1905. Its rules are similar, with some differences, such as 60-minute games and a smaller sliotar.

Scotland's **shinty** is a close cousin of hurling. Since 1896, Ireland and Scotland have played international matches under a mix of the two codes' rules.

## How it's played

The rules below follow the **GAA Official Guide Part 2**, effective from 10 March 2025, plus changes voted on in 2026 (noted where they apply).

### The pitch

Hurling is played on a grass pitch **130–145 m long and 80–90 m wide**. Gaelic football uses the same pitch.

{{diagram}}

- **Lines** cross the pitch at **13 m, 20 m, 45 m and 65 m** from each end line. The 65 m line is used only in hurling. A short halfway line is marked in the centre.
- **Arc:** A semicircle with a 13 m radius sits outside each 20 m line. In football there is also a 40 m arc, but that arc is for football only.
- **Goals:** H-shaped posts **6.5 m apart**, at least 7 m tall, with a crossbar **2.5 m** above the ground and a net below it.
- **Rectangles:** A small rectangle (14 m × 4.5 m) and a large rectangle (19 m × 13 m) are marked in front of each goal.

### Teams and positions

Each team has **15 players**: a goalkeeper and 14 outfield players. The traditional line-up is:

- Goalkeeper
- Full-back line: right corner-back, full-back, left corner-back
- Half-back line: right wing-back, centre-back, left wing-back
- Midfield: two midfielders
- Half-forward line: right wing-forward, centre-forward, left wing-forward
- Full-forward line: right corner-forward, full-forward, left corner-forward

A team may use **five substitutes** in normal time, plus extra substitutes for blood injuries or suspected head injuries.

### Scoring

- **Point:** over the crossbar between the posts. Worth **1**.
- **Goal:** under the crossbar into the net. Worth **3**.

Scores are written as goals–points. For example, 1-29 means one goal and 29 points, a total of 32.

You may not throw the ball in for a score or carry it over the goal line. You may strike the ball in flight with your hand.

**Two-point scores are for Gaelic football, not hurling.** From 2025 the GAA trialled a two-point score from outside a 40 m arc in football. At a Special Congress in October 2025 that rule became permanent from 1 January 2026. In the hurling rules, a point is still worth one, wherever it is struck from.

### Key rules

- **Lifting:** You may not pick the ball off the ground with your hand. Lift it with the hurley, or flick it up with your foot.
- **Carrying:** You may hold the ball in your hand for no more than **four steps**. To go further, balance or bounce it on the hurley (a "solo"). After catching the ball, you can bring it back into your hand only once.
- **Handpass:** You may pass with the hand, but only with a clear striking action. Throwing the ball is a foul.
- **Shoulder charge:** This is allowed shoulder to shoulder, with at least one foot on the ground, against a player who has or is playing the ball.
- **Goalkeeper:** Inside the small rectangle the goalkeeper may not be charged.
- **Restarts:** After a score or a wide, the goalkeeper restarts with a **puck-out** from the small rectangle. If a defender sends the ball wide, the attackers get a free from the **65 m line**. If the ball goes out over the sideline, the other team takes a **sideline cut** from the ground.

### Fouls and cards

**Technical fouls**, such as over-carrying, throwing the ball or picking it off the ground, give the other team a **free puck**. Defenders must stand 20 m back for a free.

**Aggressive fouls**, such as tripping, pulling down or careless use of the hurley, lead to a free and a **yellow card**. Two cautions mean a **red card**, and the player is sent off. Striking or kicking an opponent with force is a straight red.

**Penalty puck:** An aggressive foul by a defender inside the large rectangle gives a penalty. It is struck from the centre of the 20 m line, and only one defender (usually the goalkeeper) may stand on the goal line. This one-defender rule dates from 2015.

**Black card (cynical fouls):** In inter-county senior, under-20 and minor games, there is an extra rule. If a player pulls down, trips or recklessly hurls an attacker who has a goal-scoring chance, the attacking team gets a penalty. The offender gets a **black card** and **10 minutes in the sin bin**. Congress first approved this sin-bin rule for hurling on a trial basis in 2021.

**Dissent:** The 2025 rulebook moved a free 13 m forward when a player showed dissent. At its February 2026 Congress, the GAA voted to bring hurling into line with football's dissent rules. A free can now be moved **30 m** forward (it is 50 m in football).

### Officials

A senior match has a **referee**, two **linesmen** and **four umpires**, two at each goal. Inter-county senior games add a **sideline official**, who handles substitutions.

The umpires signal scores with flags: **green for a goal** and **white for a point**. They cross their arms above their heads for a wide. At Croke Park and some other approved grounds, the **Hawk-Eye** system can check whether a ball went over the bar or wide.

### Match length

- **Inter-county senior** championship and league games: **70 minutes**, in two halves of 35.
- **Most other games:** 60 minutes, in two halves of 30. Younger grades can be shorter.
- **Extra time:** two periods of 10 minutes. Drawn knockout games then go to a "winner on the day" method set by the GAA.

### Rules being trialled

In September 2026 the GAA's Hurling Expert Advisory Group recommended 24 rule changes. They will be tested in third-level (Higher Education) league games from October 2026.

The proposals include six steps instead of four, no handpass straight after another handpass, and two points for a sideline cut struck over the bar. **These are trials only.** They are not part of the standard rules unless the GAA adopts them later.

## Equipment

- **Hurley (camán):** A curved wooden stick, usually made of ash. Hurleys are typically about 61–91 cm (24–36 in) long, chosen to suit the player's height. The flat, wide end is called the *bas*. A goalkeeper's bas is much bigger. Metal bands on the bas must be taped over in games up to under-12.
- **Sliotar:** A cork-centred ball with a leather cover and a raised rib. It must be **69–72 mm** across and weigh **110–116 g**, and match balls carry a GAA mark of approval. White was traditional, and yellow balls are now widely used. The rules also allow a smart chip in the sliotar to show it is genuine.
- **Helmet:** A helmet with a **face guard** is compulsory in all hurling games and training. The rule has applied to all grades since **1 January 2010** (minor and under-21 grades began in 2009). Helmets must meet the Irish standard IS:355 and must not be altered.
- **Kit:** County or club jersey, shorts and socks. The goalkeeper wears a different-coloured jersey.

## Where it's played today

The **GAA** governs hurling worldwide from Croke Park in Dublin. Clubs exist across Ireland and in Britain, North America, Europe, Australia and Asia.

- **All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship:** The top inter-county competition. The winners lift the **Liam MacCarthy Cup**, first presented in 1923. Liam MacCarthy was a London-born son of Irish parents who helped found the London GAA board. Croke Park has hosted almost every final since 1910.
- **Provincial championships:** Notably Munster and Leinster.
- **National Hurling League:** The spring inter-county league.
- **Club championships:** County titles lead to the All-Ireland Club Championship.
- **Camogie:** The Camogie Association runs the women's game. Its All-Ireland winners receive the O'Duffy Cup.

After the 2026 final, Kilkenny still lead the roll of honour with **36** All-Ireland senior titles. Cork have **30**, Tipperary **29** (including 2025) and Limerick **13** (including 2026).

## Famous moments and players

- **Christy Ring (Cork, 1920–1979):** Won **eight** All-Ireland senior medals with Cork and 18 Railway Cup medals with Munster. He is often named among the greatest hurlers. Source: [Christy Ring - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christy_Ring).
- **Henry Shefflin (Kilkenny):** Won **10** All-Ireland senior medals, more than any other player, along with 11 All Stars. He is the only three-time Hurler of the Year (2002, 2006, 2012). Source: [Henry Shefflin - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Shefflin).
- **Kilkenny's four in a row (2006–2009):** Kilkenny won four finals in a row. They beat Cork, Limerick, Waterford and Tipperary. Source: [List of All-Ireland SHC finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_All-Ireland_Senior_Hurling_Championship_finals).
- **Limerick's modern era:** Limerick won four finals in a row from 2020 to 2023 and added another in 2026, beating Galway 1-29 to 1-18. Source: [List of All-Ireland SHC finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_All-Ireland_Senior_Hurling_Championship_finals).
- **London, 1901:** London is the only team from outside Ireland to win the All-Ireland senior title. Source: [Hurling - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurling).
- **An Olympic cameo, 1904:** Hurling was a demonstration sport at the St. Louis Olympics. Innisfails of St. Louis beat the Fenians of Chicago. Source: [Hurling - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurling).
