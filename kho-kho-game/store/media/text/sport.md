# The sport: kho kho

Kho kho is a team game of tag from India, played for generations in villages, schools and stadiums across South Asia.
Its rules were first written down in Pune in the early twentieth century, and today it is played in national
championships, professional leagues and, since 2025, at a world championship.

Two teams take turns to chase and to defend. The chasing team kneels in a row of squares down the middle of the
field, each player facing the opposite way to the one beside him, with a pole at each end. One chaser, the
"active chaser", is on his feet. He may run only in the direction he first chose, may not cross the central lane
between the squares, and can change direction only by going round a pole.

To get round those limits he passes the chase on: he touches a seated team-mate on the back and calls "kho!". That
player rises and becomes the chaser, and the first sits down in his place. A good side passes the kho quickly up and
down the line, closing in from both sides.

The defenders come on three at a time and try to stay in for as long as they can. A defender is out when the active
chaser touches him, or when he steps outside the field. The chasing side scores for every defender it puts out,
and after each side has chased twice, the side with more points wins.
