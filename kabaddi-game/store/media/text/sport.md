# What is kabaddi?

Kabaddi is a contact team sport from South Asia, played for centuries in villages across India, Bangladesh, Nepal,
Pakistan and Sri Lanka. It is the national sport of Bangladesh and a state sport in several Indian states.

Two teams of seven face each other across a court split by a midline. Teams take turns to send one player, the
raider, into the other half. Traditionally the raider does it on a single breath, chanting "kabaddi, kabaddi" (the
cant) to show they have not breathed in; in the professional game a 30-second clock is used instead. The raider
scores a point for each defender they touch and for crossing the bonus line, but only if they make it back over the
midline. The defenders work together to stop that: if they hold the raider until the raid is over, the defence
scores. Players who are out leave the court and come back as their team scores, and a team that loses all seven
concedes extra points for an "all out".

The sport joined the Asian Games in 1990, with a women's event since 2010, and has its own World Cups. Since the
Pro Kabaddi League began in India in 2014, matches have drawn television audiences in the hundreds of millions, and
the sport is now played on mats in arenas as well as on the earth and sand courts where it grew up.
